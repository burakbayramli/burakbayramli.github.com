Beef bourguignon

Ingredients

  • Beef chuck
  • 1 bottle red wine
  • Onions, chopped
  • A few cloves of garlic, smashed
  • Carrots, chopped (plus a few extra for finishing)
  • 1 bouquet garni (tied herbs)
  • Bacon lardons
  • Neutral or high smoke point oil
  • Salt and fresh cracked black pepper
  • Tomato paste
  • Flour
  • Splash of brandy (optional)
  • Beef stock
  • Mushrooms
  • Pearl onions
  • Butter
  • Bread (cut into heart shapes for croutons)
  • Cornstarch and warm water (optional, for thickening)
  • Tiny splash of sherry vinegar (optional, for acidity)
  • Red currant jelly (optional, for sweetness)
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Mashed potatoes / pomme purée (for serving)

    • Description

    Combine the beef chuck, chopped onions, smashed garlic, chopped carrots, and bouquet garni in a bowl or container, and cover completely with a bottle of red wine. Refrigerate and let marinate overnight.

    Remove the beef from the marinade and set aside. Fish out the vegetables and bouquet garni from the red wine, reserving both the vegetables and the wine separately.

    In a heavy ovenproof pot over medium-high heat, cook the bacon lardons (adding a small splash of oil if needed to help render) until crispy. Use a slotted spoon to remove the crispy bacon, leaving the rendered fat in the pot.

    Season the marinated beef with salt and fresh cracked pepper, then sear it in the hot bacon fat, pressing down for even contact until a deep golden-brown crust forms. Watch carefully as the sugars from the wine can brown quickly. Remove the beef and set aside.

    Add the reserved marinade vegetables to the pot and stir to soften, scraping up all the browned bits from the bottom until deeply golden and caramelized.

    Add the tomato paste and stir to caramelize until it releases an orange oil. Lower heat to medium, add the flour, and cook for a couple of minutes to cook out the raw flour taste.

    Optionally add a splash of brandy, then deglaze the pot with the reserved red wine, scraping down the sides and bottom. Simmer until the wine is reduced by about half.

    Pour in the beef stock and return the seared beef (along with the bouquet garni) to the pot. Cover the surface with parchment paper so the top of the beef does not dry out, place the lid on, and bake in the oven at 375 °F (190 °C) for 3 hours, until the beef is tender and flakes easily without completely falling apart.

    While the beef braises, heat a little high smoke point oil in a pan over medium heat. Add the mushrooms and pearl onions and sauté until golden brown. Add a knob of butter and cook until slightly softened, then toss in the reserved crispy bacon to coat. Use a slotted spoon to set the garnish aside, leaving the flavorful oil in the pan.

    In the same pan over low-medium heat, toast the heart-shaped bread croutons on each side until golden brown, then transfer to a wire-rack-lined baking sheet.

    Once the beef is done, remove and discard the bouquet garni. Carefully lift the tender beef out of the pot using a wide spatula or slotted spoon so it stays intact, and set aside.

    Check the consistency of the sauce. If too thin, either simmer to reduce it (being mindful of salt concentration) or stir in a slurry of cornstarch and warm water bit by bit, simmering for a few minutes until glossy and thick enough to coat the pan. Taste and adjust with a tiny splash of sherry vinegar for acidity or a little red currant jelly for sweetness, if desired. Finish by mounting the sauce with cold butter.

    Return the beef (and any resting juices), the sautéed mushroom-onion-bacon garnish, and a few lightly cooked carrots to the pot, spooning the glossy sauce over the beef and sprinkling with chopped parsley. (Optionally dip the edges of the toasted bread hearts into the sauce and chopped parsley.)

    Serve hot over mashed potatoes (pomme purée), surrounded by the vegetables, generous spoonfuls of sauce, and the heart-shaped croutons.
